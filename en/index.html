Nothing was replaced

Each stage stacks on top of the previous ones. Behind the word “AI” today sit all of these layers at once, each with its own uses.

  1. Physical AI and roboticsUnder construction: robotaxis, humanoids, general-purpose arms.
  2. Agentic AIActing: searching, coding, filling in forms, driving a browser.
  3. Generative AIWriting, summarizing, translating, generating images and voices.
  4. TransformersEmbeddings, semantic search, text classification.
  5. Deep learningImage and speech recognition, OCR, vision for robots and cars.
  6. Machine learningPrediction, scoring, recommendations, anomaly detection.
  7. Symbolic AIRule engines, planning, route calculation.

On the modality side, text, image, sound, video and action now run through a single model. Robots are following, but remain in labs and pilots: Moravec’s paradox still holds, and walking and grasping remain harder than writing. That is probably the next stage.

Specialized models are often still more reliable and cheaper for a specific task: Whisper for transcription, a classic machine learning model for anomaly detection.

About this timeline

This is one proposed reading of the history of AI, not an inventory. The milestones chosen are those that changed what the word “AI” refers to, plus failures, controversies and public decisions. The prologue gathers intellectual and technical roots, drawn in part from Wikipedia’s timeline of AI: not AI yet, but ideas it would later take up. Recent years are denser: the acceleration is real, but history has not yet separated true turning points from mere launches.

The banner is an interpretation. A year’s “dominant technique” is an editorial choice, and the line linking modalities means that models can handle them together, not that a single model does everything at every company. Stage 7 is a projection, not an observation.

Each milestone links first to Wikipedia, then to the official announcement or a press article, and to the scientific paper when there is one. Figures put forward by companies are flagged as such.

Known biases: American players and large private labs remain overrepresented, and the story told here is primarily a technical one. This timeline was made with the help of Claude, Anthropic’s model, which may have overweighted its own maker’s milestones; their number was reduced accordingly.
